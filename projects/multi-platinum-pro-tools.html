Book · Focal Press, 2006

Multi-Platinum Pro Tools

Advanced Editing, Pocketing and Autotuning Techniques. With Brady Barnett. Focal Press, 2006. Eight chapters, over 300 colour illustrations, and a DVD carrying the actual session. Publisher's page.

What it is about

Two techniques, mostly. Pocketing is the business of moving recorded performances against each other until a band that played slightly apart sounds like a band that played together. Autotuning — pitch correction — is the same operation applied to intonation rather than time.

Both were, in 2006, things that every serious Nashville session was doing and almost nobody was writing down. The techniques were passed between engineers verbally, in rooms, and the published literature covered the software’s features rather than what working people actually did with them. This book wrote down the practice.

How it is built

Eight chapters, following one session in order rather than one feature at a time. Setting up your preferences so the software stops fighting you. Building the pocket from the drums up. Beat Detective. Getting the bass player on time. Locking up the acoustics. Three different electric guitars, which behave like three different instruments. Then autotuning the lead and backing vocals.

Chapter 6 contains a section called “A bad analogy: Building our pocket”. I would like it on the record that a major academic publisher printed that, and has kept it in the catalogue for twenty years. :)

The DVD

It shipped with a DVD carrying a real Nashville session, so a reader could open the same material the book was describing and do the edits themselves rather than read about somebody else doing them. Both a pre-edited version and the final mix are on there, which is the part that matters: you can hear the gap the techniques close.

That was the whole design. A technique you have only read about is not a technique you have.

Why anyone cared

Because the argument about it was already public. MSNBC put me in front of a camera to answer whether any of this counted as cheating, which is the version of the question the general audience had. The book is the version for the people doing it, and it takes the same position: these are tools for making a record sound like the performance everyone in the room believed they gave.

Brady Barnett

Brady is an independent producer and recording engineer and a co-owner of Of Sound Mind Productions in Nashville. He had cut gold and platinum records across rock, pop, country and contemporary Christian, and he was fast in a way that people came out of his classes talking about.

The book exists because of that, not because of me. I was the one who noticed that a room full of students would queue for his class and that nothing he knew had ever been written down.
